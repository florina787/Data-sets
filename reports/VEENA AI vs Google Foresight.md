# VEENA Wins Breadth, Foresight Wins Depth

VEENA.AI is **broader, more open and more portable** than Google AI Edge Foresight, but on current evidence it is not "way better." It is better on a different axis. VEENA has several real advantages. It is GPL-3.0 source that anyone can audit. It builds for Windows, macOS and Linux. It analyses the screen, a feature Foresight lacks entirely. It has explicit retention and redaction controls, it lets users swap models, and it has no telemetry pipeline that anyone has found. Foresight wins on the job both products share, the live meeting. It officially captures system audio from any meeting app on a Mac, enriches shorthand notes from the transcript, connects to Google Drive and Calendar, and runs much stronger Apache-2.0 models (Gemma 4 E4B/12B and EmbeddingGemma 2) on Apple Silicon. Hands-on testers found it held up through a 1.5-hour meeting. Several claims made for VEENA do not survive the evidence and need correcting. Foresight *does* officially promise "exact citations." VEENA's mid-tier default text model, Qwen2.5-3B, is under the Qwen Research License, not Apache 2.0. VEENA's "works offline" claim has never been tested with networking disabled. VEENA's Meeting Copilot and Interview Practice have only been exercised against a simulated model. No one has run the two apps side by side on the same Mac. Both products are weeks-old experiments: VEENA is a v0.1 single-author project, and Foresight is a Google developer showcase. So the honest verdict is "different strengths, unproven head-to-head," and VEENA can earn a stronger claim with four or five concrete changes.

## How the evidence was graded

Every claim below carries one of five grades. **A** means an automated test with real models; for VEENA these ran only on Linux, on a 4-vCPU machine with no GPU. **B** means an automated test with simulated models, or unit/integration tests. **C** means an independent hands-on or technical observation; for Foresight, nearly all of this comes from two *competitor* testers (talat and Recall) and from inspection of the installer bundle. **D** means the claim appears only in the vendor's own documents. **E** means nothing has verified it, or there are no data at all. The grades matter because the two products are documented in very different ways. VEENA's repository rates its own limitations unusually frankly, but its strongest test evidence comes from one small Linux VM ([VEENA BENCHMARKS.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/BENCHMARKS.md)). Foresight's evidence is mostly Google marketing copy, plus bundle strings and a few days of competitor testing ([talat](https://talat.app/blog/google-ai-edge-foresight); [Recall](https://www.recall.it/post/google-ai-edge-foresight-vs-a-second-brain)). Neither side has a quality benchmark for answers, meeting notes or transcription word-error rate.

The table shows where each product's centre of gravity lies. VEENA is a general desktop copilot, and meetings are one mode among several. Its unique features are **screen intelligence, redaction, governance checks, interview practice and three-OS packaging**. Foresight is a narrowly focused meeting companion. Its unique strength is the meeting loop itself: **system-audio capture, shorthand enrichment, and Drive/Calendar context**. That loop is exactly where VEENA's evidence is weakest. On a Mac, VEENA cannot capture the remote side of a Zoom or Meet call at all, because loopback is enabled only when `process.platform === "win32"` ([main.ts](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/apps/desktop/electron/main.ts)). Its Meeting Copilot "not yet run end to end with a real model" ([README.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/README.md)). Foresight has bugs of its own. One tester saw Live Enhance stay inert until toggled, chat apologise three times when asked for a nine-minute summary, and remote speech duplicated when laptop speakers were used instead of headphones ([talat](https://talat.app/blog/google-ai-edge-foresight)). Recall saw chat miss a factual question about the meeting ([Recall](https://www.recall.it/post/google-ai-edge-foresight-vs-a-second-brain)). Still, a buggy feature that real users have exercised beats a feature that has only been simulated.

One correction belongs here. VEENA cannot claim citations as a differentiator. Google's FAQ states that "Foresight displays proactive context cards with exact citations" ([Google product page](https://developers.google.com/frame/edge/foresight/index_36bafa421e450bd2f8ebb067988038e9b63aecb4cbea9f3ae27b664cc8b899c8.frame)), and the bundled prompt tells the model to answer "based ONLY on the provided context" with `[Source:N]` tags ([installer DMG](https://dl.google.com/foresight-mac/foresight_mac.dmg)). What VEENA can legitimately claim is the *post-hoc governance layer*. It flags citations to passages that don't exist, and retrieved evidence the answer never cited ([ARCHITECTURE.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)). Nobody has shown that this layer produces fewer hallucinations than Foresight's prompt-level grounding.

## Licensing favours VEENA's code but Foresight's models

At the application layer the contrast is stark. **VEENA is GPL-3.0-or-later** with full source ([LICENSE](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/LICENSE)). **Foresight is a closed Google binary**, "© 2026 Google LLC. All rights reserved," governed by the general Google Terms of Service, and no source has been released ([Google product page](https://developers.google.com/frame/edge/foresight/index_36bafa421e450bd2f8ebb067988038e9b63aecb4cbea9f3ae27b664cc8b899c8.frame)). Anyone can audit, fork or port VEENA. Copyleft does constrain organisations that want to embed a modified VEENA in proprietary software.

At the model layer the advantage reverses, and this is the claim most in need of correction. Gemma 4 is **Apache 2.0**, the first Gemma release under that license ([Gemma 4 E4B model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B-it); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemma-4-is-now-available-with-apache-2-0-licensing-for-the-first-time/)). EmbeddingGemma 2 is also Apache 2.0 ([EmbeddingGemma 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)). VEENA's defaults are mixed. Qwen2.5-1.5B (the Lite profile) is Apache 2.0, but **Qwen2.5-3B, the Balanced-profile default, ships under the Qwen Research License** ([Qwen2.5 blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen2.5-llm/); [USER_GUIDE.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/USER_GUIDE.md)). Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the only vision model VEENA has tested, is *probably* under the same research license as well. That point rests on the researcher's prior knowledge and was not verified, so VEENA should check it before relying on either reading. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is Apache 2.0 ([TokenMix](https://tokenmix.ai/blog/all-minilm-l6-v2-free-local-embedding-guide-2026)). VEENA's repository documents none of its third-party model licenses ([README.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/README.md)). The net result is that a commercial user of VEENA's default Balanced profile faces *more* restrictive model terms than a Foresight user does. Calling VEENA "more open" is true of the code and false of its default model stack.

## Privacy claims are strong on both sides, and both are under-tested

Both products promise local processing, and neither has proven "zero network traffic." VEENA's case rests on code. The Electron main process sets `HF_HUB_DISABLE_TELEMETRY`, `DO_NOT_TRACK` and `ANONYMIZED_TELEMETRY=False`, and cached models load with `local_files_only=True`, "so a configured app makes no network requests" ([SECURITY.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/SECURITY.md); [backend.ts](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/apps/desktop/electron/backend.ts)). **No test runs VEENA with networking disabled or captures its outbound traffic.** The author also admits that an earlier build's "offline" mode "quietly contacted the internet on startup" ([MEDIUM_ARTICLE_FORESIGHT.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/MEDIUM_ARTICLE_FORESIGHT.md)). The offline claim has regressed once already, and no regression test guards it. Grade: D, supported by code.

VEENA's stronger privacy features are the controls it puts in users' hands. These are tested. Screenshots are not persisted, and a test checks that no image files appear in the data directory. Retention purge runs on a range from 1 day to 1 year. Clear-all requires confirmation. Passwords are redacted before reaching the model ([test_api.py](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/tests/integration/test_api.py); [store.py](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/backend/memory/store.py)). The backend binds to loopback only, with a per-launch 256-bit token, and the E2E and CI smoke tests verify this ([test_security.py](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/tests/unit/test_security.py)). By contrast, Google publishes **no statement on Foresight's storage location, encryption or retention** ([Google product page](https://developers.google.com/frame/edge/foresight/index_36bafa421e450bd2f8ebb067988038e9b63aecb4cbea9f3ae27b664cc8b899c8.frame)).

Foresight's case is official but contradicted in part. Google says "All AI inference runs 100% locally" and the app is "completely offline-first," usable "in air-gapped secure facilities" ([Google product page](https://developers.google.com/frame/edge/foresight/index_36bafa421e450bd2f8ebb067988038e9b63aecb4cbea9f3ae27b664cc8b899c8.frame)). talat's log analysis found **no audio or transcript content leaving the machine**, which is consistent with the on-device inference claim. It also found **74 de-identified telemetry reports of 25 kinds in about two minutes**, starting within 2 seconds of first launch and before sign-in, with no opt-out. The reports carried meeting duration, word counts, question counts and model names. The same analysis found a background job that relaunches the app every four hours even after the user quits ([talat](https://talat.app/blog/google-ai-edge-foresight)). The bundle compiles in Firebase Analytics, although its config file sets `IS_ANALYTICS_ENABLED` to false ([installer DMG](https://dl.google.com/foresight-mac/foresight_mac.dmg)). talat is a competitor and its analysis has not been independently replicated, so treat it as grade C. On present evidence, though, **VEENA's privacy posture is more credible**: it ships no known telemetry, and its source can be audited. It is not yet *proven* better, because neither app has had a neutral packet capture.

## Model quality favours Foresight, and VEENA's speed figures cannot be compared

On vendor-reported benchmarks, Foresight's default generator is in a different class. **Gemma 4 E4B scores 69.4 on MMLU-Pro and 58.6 on GPQA Diamond**, against **43.7 and 30.3 for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct** and 32.4 and 29.8 for Qwen2.5-1.5B ([Gemma 4 E4B model card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B-it); [Qwen2.5 blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen2.5-llm/)). The comparison is imperfect. The benchmark versions differ, the two evaluations are about 18 months apart, and Gemma's scores may involve thinking mode. But a 25-point gap is too large to be an artefact. For embeddings, EmbeddingGemma 2 reports 68.46 on MTEB English v2, with 100+ languages and an 8K-token context. MiniLM sits around 56 on an unspecified MTEB version, with a usable window of about 256 tokens ([EmbeddingGemma 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2); [TokenMix](https://tokenmix.ai/blog/all-minilm-l6-v2-free-local-embedding-guide-2026)). VEENA's counterweight is footprint. E4B holds about 8B total parameters, and Foresight downloaded about **4.2 GB of models** during an 8.5-minute setup ([talat](https://talat.app/blog/google-ai-edge-foresight)). VEENA's Lite profile targets 8 GB machines with a 1.5B model ([USER_GUIDE.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/USER_GUIDE.md)). In transcription the picture flips. Foresight's speech model is English-only, and its Dart layer strips non-ASCII characters ([farsight](https://github.com/hadi-buddy/farsight)). Whisper is multilingual by design, but VEENA has tested only one synthetic English sentence. Its benchmark transcripts also show real errors, such as "two keen share a market" for a hash-map question ([latest.json](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/benchmarks/latest.json)).

VEENA's measured performance is real but narrow. On a 4-vCPU Xeon VM with no GPU, qwen2.5:1.5b gives a **warm first token at a p50 of 187 ms and generates 21.2 tok/s**, but a cold first token takes 80 s. Whisper base runs at an RTF of 0.38. Partials arrive every 2–3 s instead of the configured 0.9 s. A screenshot analysis takes **48–81 s warm and 290 s cold** ([BENCHMARKS.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/BENCHMARKS.md)). The 48–81 s vision figures come from E2E console logs and are not stored in `latest.json`, sample sizes are n = 2–3, and the 3B and 7B models were never measured ([latest.json](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/benchmarks/latest.json)). Foresight has **no published latency, RAM, CPU or battery measurements**. The closest data point is that, on an M3 with 16 GB, transcription kept up through a 1.5-hour meeting and the summary took about two more minutes ([Recall](https://www.recall.it/post/google-ai-edge-foresight-vs-a-second-brain)). The two datasets come from different hardware, models and tasks, so **any claim that one product is faster is currently unsupported**. VEENA's author acknowledges this: "I haven't run both apps side by side yet" ([MEDIUM_ARTICLE_FORESIGHT.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/MEDIUM_ARTICLE_FORESIGHT.md)).

Platform support is VEENA's clearest structural win, though a qualified one. CI builds a Windows NSIS installer, a macOS arm64 DMG and a Linux AppImage, and smoke-tests that each launches with its backend on loopback. **No model answer has been tested on Windows or macOS**, the NSIS installer has never been run, and there is no Intel Mac build ([TESTED_VERSIONS.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/TESTED_VERSIONS.md); [veena-ai-ci.yml](file:///home/user/Data-sets/.github/workflows/veena-ai-ci.yml)). The repository also contains no CI logs, so the passing status rests on the README badge. Foresight is Apple Silicon only, on macOS 14.2+, and Google has announced no Windows or Linux plans ([heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Google-s-macOS-app-Local-AI-to-capture-notes-and-work-with-files-11479591.html); [installer DMG](https://dl.google.com/foresight-mac/foresight_mac.dmg)). Community ports already exist, including an unofficial Windows port and an open-source Linux/macOS clone ([foresight-win](https://github.com/Pantheonn3D/foresight-win); [open-foresight](https://github.com/teng-lin/open-foresight)). That erodes the uniqueness of VEENA's portability.

## What VEENA must change to claim superiority credibly

Five changes would turn the "better" claim from aspiration into evidence. First, **move the defaults to Apache-licensed models**. That means Gemma 4 E2B/E4B for text, which also handles images, plus EmbeddingGemma 2 or another Apache embedder. VEENA would then match Foresight's model quality and licensing while keeping its own advantages, and its Ollama-based model switching makes the change cheap ([TESTED_VERSIONS.md](file:///home/user/Data-sets/veena-ai/docs/TESTED_VERSIONS.md)). Keep Qwen2.5-1.5B as a Lite option. Then document every model's license in the repository, and confirm Qwen2.5-VL-3B's terms or replace that model. Second, **add an offline regression test in CI**. It should run the packaged app in a network namespace with no route out, or under an egress-logging proxy, and fail on any outbound connection. This would convert grade D into grade A, and Foresight's own telemetry findings would then make it a genuine differentiator. Third, **run Meeting Copilot and Interview Practice end to end against real models**. Use multi-speaker, noisy and non-English recordings, and publish a small graded evaluation set covering WER, action-item recall and the consistency of feedback scores. Fourth, **close the system-audio gap on macOS**, using the Core Audio process-tap API that Foresight's macOS 14.2 floor suggests it relies on. Without it, VEENA cannot serve remote meetings on a Mac at all. Fifth, **run a side-by-side benchmark on one Apple Silicon Mac**. Feed both apps the same recorded meeting and the same document set, and report transcription accuracy, time to first answer, summary latency, citation correctness, peak RAM and battery drain, with n ≥ 5 per measure. Signed builds, an auto-updater and a shorthand-to-notes mode would further narrow Foresight's lead on polish.

## Conclusion

The comparison exposes a mismatch between what each product is *for* and what each has *proven*. Foresight is narrow but has been tested in real meetings, while VEENA is broad but mostly tested with simulated models in the one area where the two compete. VEENA's real moat is not raw capability. It is **auditability**: an open codebase whose privacy and offline claims *can* be tested, against a closed binary whose telemetry testers found only by reading logs. That moat is worth something only once VEENA runs the tests. If it ships a networking-disabled CI check and Apache-licensed Gemma 4 defaults, VEENA would hold the one position Foresight structurally cannot take: verifiably private, cross-platform, and as capable at the model level.

Two unknowns could change the verdict, and both should be resolved before anyone claims superiority. The first is whether Foresight's telemetry finding holds up under a neutral packet capture. The second is how VEENA's CPU-bound pipeline, especially its roughly one-minute screenshot analysis, performs on Apple Silicon against Foresight's LiteRT/Metal stack. Until the side-by-side benchmark exists, "way better" is a hypothesis, not a finding.
